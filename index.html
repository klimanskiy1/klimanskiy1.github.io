Questions

What owners ask before saying yes

Why would you build it for free?

Because most of the page-building is done by a tool I wrote. A 35-page site costs me a couple of days, not a month, and a preview you can click through is a better pitch than a proposal document. If you go live, I get paid; if you don't, I have lost two days and you have lost nothing.

Will my text, prices and photos change?

No. The design changes; the facts do not. If your site lists 95 dive sites and 42 courses, the rebuilt one lists 95 dive sites and 42 courses with the same prices. Anything you want changed, you tell me, and it changes in one place.

What happens to my Google ranking, my domain and my email?

The site stays on your domain. Page addresses are kept where they exist, and old addresses redirect to new ones, so links from Google, TripAdvisor and booking platforms keep working. The files Google needs are generated with the site. Nothing goes live until you say so, so there is no downtime.

Email is untouched. Going live changes only the website record of your domain; mail stays exactly where it is.

Can I edit the site myself later? What do changes cost?

Yes. Text and prices live in plain files, not scattered across a page builder; change a price once and every page and language follows. I show you how in the walkthrough.

If you would rather not touch it: small text and price changes, send them over, no charge. Bigger changes, a new section or a new language, I quote first.

What about bookings, forms, maps, the shop?

Booking widgets such as FareHarbor, maps, WhatsApp buttons and payment links are kept as they are. Contact forms work without a server. Shop catalogues can be plain files too: the California Scuba Center rebuild carries 1,277 products with filters, search and sorting, and no database.

Why is it so much faster?

A page builder ships dozens of scripts and plugins on every page whether they are used or not. A static site ships the page: one stylesheet, a few kilobytes of script, images sized for the screen they are shown on. That is why the home pages above got two to twenty-five times lighter, and why the hosting is free.

Which languages can it have?

Any. The Vietnamese rebuilds serve English, Russian, Vietnamese, Korean and Chinese, with the language switch in the header and every page available in every language.

What if I don't like the preview?

Then you owe nothing and the preview stays private; it is hidden from Google and never listed anywhere. If it is close but not right, tell me what is off. Corrections are part of the deal, not an extra.

What if you disappear in a year?

Everything is plain files on your own hosting, and you have the tool that builds them. Any developer can pick it up; there is no account with me that the site depends on.

Who am I dealing with?

One person, no agency. Matvey Klimanskiy, full-stack developer, based in Nha Trang, working remotely in English or Russian. I started on the backend and moved to the front end after watching resort websites fail to load on a phone with weak Wi-Fi. I also build products of my own, listed further down, which is where the engineering habits come from.

How I work

The same rules for a client site and for my own products

Nothing gets invented. A rebuilt site carries the business's own text, prices, courses and photos exactly. The design changes; the facts do not.

Content as data, pages as code. Every site ships with a small generator: change a price in one file and every page, language and sitemap follows. No page builder, no plugins, no database to keep alive.

A rule that exists only in prose is a suggestion. In my own products the constraints live in a guard linter and pre-commit hooks, mirrored in CI. WipeSquad has sixteen of them, from “identity comes only from the server session” to “no copyleft in server code”.

Agents type; I accept. I use AI coding agents heavily and say so. They never review their own output, a security pass runs separately, and my own eyes go over the result before anything ships or goes to a client.
